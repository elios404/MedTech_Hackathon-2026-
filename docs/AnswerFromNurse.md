1.           How is waste normally generated and disposed of during a procedure?

For example, are items usually thrown away one by one as they are used or opened, or are multiple items sometimes collected together and disposed of at once?

 

The majority of the items producing waste are opened at the beginning of the case, with additional items opened during the case as required.  We have different bins within the operating theatre, so waste is disposed of as it is produced.  All waste must remain in the theatre for the duration of the case (just in case there is a problem with the count).

 

2.           Who normally disposes of the waste?

Is it mainly nurses, theatre technicians, surgeons, or different staff depending on the stage of the procedure?

 

It is mainly nursing staff who dispose of waste but surgeons will dispose of their own gowns / gloves.

 

3.           At what stage is most waste generated?

For example, is a lot of waste generated while preparing the theatre and opening sterile packaging before the patient arrives, during the procedure itself, or during clean-up afterwards?

 

The majority of waste is generated at the beginning of the case but waste is not removed until the end of the case.

 

4.           How do staff decide which bin to use?

Are general waste, clinical/infectious waste and recyclable waste bins located close together? Do staff ever find it difficult or inconvenient to decide which bin an item should go into, particularly during busy or high-pressure situations?

 

Bins are colour coded to try to help staff identify correct waste streams.  Medical waste and Sharps are yellow, dry waste has a brown lid, recycling is often white with clear identification of which products it takes.  Staff can find it very difficult to decide which bin to use, especially during busy periods or high pressure situations.  There are often mistakes eg contamination of recycling because of this.

 

5.           How is contaminated waste handled?

For example, are blood-contaminated materials normally disposed of individually as they are used, or are they sometimes collected together and placed into a bag/container later?

 

All items that are contaminated with bodily fluids are disposed of through the yellow medical waste bins.  Any body fluids (eg blood within suction cannisters) are disposed via a special waste pump system.

 

6.           Once clinical waste has been placed into a bag or container, is it ever opened or sorted again?

Or is it sealed and treated as clinical waste from that point onwards?

 

If we have an incorrect count we have to open all waste to search for the item we have lost eg packs or swabs. 

 

7.           What happens when the procedure is finished?

Who closes or seals the waste bags, who removes them from the theatre, and what happens to them afterwards?

 

The PSSA (patient support orderly) removes the waste from the area after the case and takes it to the disposal room.

 

8.           From your experience, what makes correct waste segregation difficult in an operating theatre?

Is it lack of knowledge, unclear categories, time pressure, workload, the location/design of bins, or something else?

 

All of the above can contribute, if waste is made of a single product eg paper then it is easy to categorise but if they are made of multiple products eg paper, plastic and metal that cannot be broken down then that makes things more complicated.  Add in that you need to make quick decisions because time is limited and that you are relying on a person’s conscience to make the right decision.   This can result in inappropriate choices.  There are also lots of bins in theatre, all various shapes, sizes and colours in different locations.

 

9.           If the waste system could be improved, what would make it easier for clinical staff?

Ideally, I'm interested in solutions that would reduce the amount of thinking or extra work required from nurses and other clinical staff rather than adding another task to their workload.

 

Better storage solutions for waste reducing the footprint in theatre

Better ways of identifying which stream for which waste item
